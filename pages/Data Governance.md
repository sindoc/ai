- **Data Governance** is the set of policies, processes, standards, roles, and metrics that ensure the effective and efficient use of information in enabling an organisation to achieve its goals.
- Wikipedia: "Data governance is a term used on both a macro and a micro level. The former is a political concept and forms part of international relations and Internet governance; the latter is a data management concept and forms part of corporate/organisational data governance."
- ## Organisational Data Governance
	- In the organisational context, data governance covers:
		- **Data ownership** — who is accountable for a data asset's quality and appropriate use
		- **Data quality** — standards, rules, and monitoring (see [[AI Governance/Data Quality]])
		- **Data lineage** — tracking data from source through transformation to consumption
		- **Access control** — who may read, modify, or delete data assets
		- **Retention and disposal** — lifecycle rules for data records
- ## Relationship to AI Governance
	- AI systems consume and produce data; [[AI Governance]] cannot be effective without sound data governance upstream.
	- The [[Collibra Operating Model]] treats Data Governance and AI Governance as linked domains within a single community structure.
