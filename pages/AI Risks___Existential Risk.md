- **Existential Risk from AI** refers to scenarios in which advanced AI systems cause harm at civilisational scale — outcomes that are either irreversible or fatal to humanity's long-term potential.
- Wikipedia: "Existential risk from artificial intelligence, or AI x-risk, refers to the idea that substantial progress in artificial general intelligence (AGI) and artificial superintelligence (ASI) could lead to human extinction or an irreversible global catastrophe."
- ## Key Pathways
	- **Misaligned superintelligence** — an AI that achieves general or super-human capabilities pursues goals misaligned with human values at a speed and scale humans cannot counteract
	- **Autonomous weapons** — AI-enabled weapons systems making lethal decisions without meaningful human control
	- **Power concentration** — a small group using advanced AI to seize unprecedented societal control
	- **Infrastructure dependency cascade** — AI systems managing critical infrastructure fail or are compromised simultaneously
- ## Relationship to [[AI alignment]]
	- Most existential risk narratives assume a failure of alignment: the AI is capable enough to be dangerous but not aligned enough to be safe.
	- The alignment field addresses this through value learning, constitutional AI, debate, and interpretability research.
- ## Governance Context
	- Existential risk is the rationale behind the most stringent requirements applied to [[AI as an Autonomous Agent (AI Independence)]] deployments.
	- The [[AI Governance]] frameworks most engaged with x-risk: UK AI Safety Institute evaluations, US Executive Order on Safe AI, EU AI Act prohibited-use provisions.
