title:: Human Value Drift
asset-code:: HVDR
public:: true

- **Human Value Drift** is the gradual, often imperceptible shift in a human's values, judgements, or preferences that results from sustained exposure to AI-mediated environments, AI-generated outputs, or AI-shaped feedback loops.
- ## Definition
	- Unlike abrupt value changes (e.g. from a single persuasive argument), value drift occurs over time as the human's cognitive environment is incrementally shaped by systems that optimise for engagement, agreement, or efficiency — rather than for the human's long-term interests or their society's values.
	- Value drift is distinct from **[[Concept Drift]]** (the statistical phenomenon in ML where data distributions shift) — though both involve drift from a baseline. Human value drift is an *output* risk, not a data distribution problem.
- ## Mechanisms
	- **Recommendation loop reinforcement** — AI systems that surface content matching the user's existing preferences gradually narrow the user's information diet
	- **Skill atrophy** — reliance on AI for tasks the human once performed creates progressive dependency (see [[AI as an Augmenter (Human-AI Symbiosis)]])
	- **Moral licensing** — delegating ethical decisions to AI ("the algorithm decided") can erode the human's own moral reasoning
	- **Framing effects** — AI-generated text consistently framing issues one way can shift how the human frames those issues over time
- ## Governance Response
	- **Drift monitoring** — periodic assessment of human decision quality in controlled AI-off conditions
	- **Diversity requirements** — mandating exposure to varied AI outputs and human sources
	- **Skill retention audits** — ensuring professionals maintain independent competence in AI-assisted domains
